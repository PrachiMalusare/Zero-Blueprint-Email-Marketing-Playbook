# Testing plan

## Testing principle

Test one meaningful variable at a time and judge it by the action it is meant to influence. A subject line may influence initial attention; a CTA may influence replies; proof and offer clarity may influence applications and enrollment.

## Test order

1. **Audience fit:** Which segment produces the highest proportion of qualified replies?
2. **Problem framing:** Which real problem creates the strongest suitable response?
3. **CTA:** Does a direct reply, short fit check or information-session invitation create better conversations?
4. **Proof:** Which mentor, outcome or experience resolves the dominant objection?
5. **Timing:** Which interval maintains momentum without increasing negative signals?

## Decision table

| Test | Primary measure | Guardrail | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Segment A vs Segment B | Qualified reply rate | Complaints and unsubscribes | Scale the segment producing better-fit conversations |
| CTA variation | Reply-to-conversation conversion | Negative replies | Keep the CTA that creates useful next steps |
| Proof emphasis | Application conversion | Sales objections | Use proof that reduces the named concern |
| Timing interval | Time to qualified response | Unsubscribe rate | Choose the cadence balancing speed and trust |

## Honest analysis

- Define the hypothesis before launch.
- Record sample size and campaign dates.
- Avoid declaring a winner from a few responses.
- Keep deliverability, audience source and send timing comparable where possible.
- Record inconclusive and failed tests in the [experiment log](../templates/experiment-log.csv).
- Do not optimize a proxy metric when the business outcome moves in the opposite direction.
