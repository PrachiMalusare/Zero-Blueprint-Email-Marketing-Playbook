# Email marketing strategy

## Objective

Generate qualified conversations and enroll suitable participants in the Zero+ Impact Blueprint while protecting trust with the existing audience.

## 1. Build the right audience before sending

Avoid treating the full database as one group. Start with people whose role, interests and current situation fit the program.

### Priority segments

1. Sustainability, ESG, materials, supply-chain and social-impact leaders.
2. Senior managers asked to lead transformation without a clear framework.
3. Aspiring leaders already engaging with sustainability or humane-leadership content.
4. Past event attendees, community members and warm professional contacts.
5. Organizational sponsors who may enroll a team member.

Use the [audience matrix](../templates/audience-matrix.csv) to record each segment's problem, desired change, evidence requirement and conversion path.

### Entry rules

- Use permission-based contacts and respect suppression lists.
- Exclude confirmed participants from acquisition emails.
- Separate recent, engaged contacts from older contacts that may need re-permission.
- Tag role, organization, geography, relationship, engagement source and likely buying authority.

## 2. Use a behavior-led sequence

The preserved sequence can remain intact while automation controls who receives the next message.

### Recommended flow

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Eligible contact enters campaign | Send Email 1 |
| Replies or requests information | Pause automation and assign a human follow-up |
| No reply after 2-3 business days | Send the next email |
| Visits the program or application page repeatedly | Flag for a personal, context-aware follow-up |
| Begins an application but does not finish | Send one helpful reminder addressing completion friction |
| Enrolls | Stop acquisition sequence and begin onboarding |
| Declines or opts out | Suppress immediately |

Suggested spacing is a starting hypothesis, not a universal rule. Adjust it using response quality, complaint signals and sales capacity.

## 3. Connect every email to a low-friction next step

The current reply-based CTA is appropriate for a high-consideration coaching program because it opens a conversation. Operationally, replies should enter a clear process:

1. Acknowledge the reply quickly during business hours.
2. Classify the contact as individual prospect, team sponsor, partner or not currently suitable.
3. Ask only the minimum questions needed to assess fit.
4. Offer a short conversation or provide the relevant information.
5. Record outcome, objection, owner and next action in the CRM.

The funnel should be visible from email entry through reply, qualified conversation, application, enrollment and revenue.

## 4. Add human follow-up at moments of intent

Automation should create timely human action, not replace it. Personal follow-up is most useful when someone:

- Replies with a substantive question.
- Revisits the offer or application page.
- Forwards the opportunity to a colleague or asks about team participation.
- Attends a related event or webinar.
- Starts but does not complete enrollment.

A short personal note should reference the recipient's situation and answer the next decision question. Avoid pretending that an automated message is individually written.

## 5. Use supporting channels selectively

Email can work alongside:

- LinkedIn posts from the program leaders that explain one idea from the curriculum.
- Personal LinkedIn outreach to warm, relevant contacts after a meaningful signal.
- A live information session or Q&A for people who need more context.
- Partner introductions from sustainability, leadership and industry communities.
- A concise landing page covering fit, outcomes, format, proof, dates and next steps.

These channels should reinforce the same promise and terminology found in the source sequence.

## 6. Measure movement, not attention alone

### Primary measures

- Qualified reply rate.
- Reply-to-conversation conversion.
- Conversation-to-application conversion.
- Application completion rate.
- Application-to-enrollment conversion.
- Revenue and qualified pipeline influenced by the campaign.
- Time from first send to qualified conversation and enrollment.
- Unsubscribe, complaint and negative-reply rates.

### Diagnostic measures

- Delivery and bounce rate.
- Clicks to high-intent pages.
- Email-specific reply themes and objections.
- Open rate only as a limited diagnostic because privacy controls and image loading reduce its reliability.

Review results by audience segment and source. A campaign with fewer replies can outperform a larger one if those replies produce more suitable participants.

## 7. Build the post-enrollment journey

Acquisition ends when someone enrolls; relationship development does not.

Create a separate onboarding flow that confirms expectations, introduces the learning journey, collects essential context and helps the participant achieve an early win. Track onboarding completion, attendance, participation, milestone progress, referrals and testimonial readiness.

## Recommended operating rhythm

- Review replies and intent signals daily during launch.
- Review funnel movement and objections twice weekly.
- Make only urgent corrections while a test is active.
- Conduct a full cohort review after the enrollment window.
- Feed sales conversations and participant feedback into the next campaign brief.
